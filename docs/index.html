One server. Every room. Your speakers.

sonn collects your music — local library, Spotify, Apple Music, Tidal, radio, even the turntable — and plays it in sync on the speakers you already own, over almost any protocol they speak. Or skip the rooms entirely and serve your content to every app in the house.

one server, two shapes

Content server, or whole-home system. Or both.

A fresh install starts with no zones and nothing shared. What you switch on decides what it becomes — and you can change your mind later without starting over.

01 / content only

Serve your music to everything

Point your library, streaming accounts and radio at sonn, switch on how you want to reach it, and every app and device in the house can browse and play — no rooms to configure, nothing to manage per device.

  • DLNA / UPnP MediaServer — smart TVs, receivers, network speakers
  • Subsonic API — Symfonium, Amperfy, DSub and friends
  • Network drive (WebDAV) — drag albums in with Finder or Explorer
  • Built-in web player, from any browser on the network

Streaming services included: a DLNA TV can browse your Apple Music or Tidal account straight from the couch.

02 / multiroom

Or own the rooms yourself

Add fixed zones — up to 24, one per room — and sonn drives the speakers directly: named rooms, synchronized groups, one place to control the whole house.

  • Central control from the web player, Admin UI or a Loxone app
  • Group any rooms to play in perfect sync
  • Per-zone equalizer, volume limits, favorites and recents
  • Amp power switching over GPIO or USB relay, per zone or shared

With the Loxone integration on, the Miniserver supplies the zones — sonn behaves as a full Loxone Audio Server.

the signal path

Your file, unchanged, at the speaker.

bit-perfect

Sendspin negotiates the format per track, so the server follows the source: the file's own samples travel over the network and the DAC clocks them at the rate they were recorded at. Nothing in the chain gets a vote.

This is not a diagram we drew for the website — it is the technical view the web player shows for every zone, live: the source format, every stage that touched the audio, and the verdict. Bit-perfect, untouched, or altered — with the reason. And if you just want music: use whatever output you already have. All of it works, all of it sounds good, and none of it needs a thought about sample rates.

content

Everything you listen to, in one place.

Every source is available to every consumer of the server — the web player, Loxone, DLNA devices and Subsonic apps alike. Configure it once, reach it from everywhere.

Local library

Folders and network shares, indexed by tags into albums, artists and tracks — with cover art, and artist pictures fetched automatically.

  • Incremental indexing — a dropped file just appears
  • Add music by drag-and-drop, or over the network drive
  • Browse, search and manage from the Admin UI

Streaming services

Spotify, Apple Music, Tidal, Deezer, YouTube Music, YouTube, SoundCloud — and Music Assistant as a source, exposing its own providers.

  • Several accounts of one service can coexist
  • Each account is its own browsable service
  • Served on to Loxone, DLNA and Subsonic too

Radio & line-in

The TuneIn directory, Radio Paradise, your own streams — plus the turntable or CD player, through any zone in the house.

  • Line-in with optional Shazam track recognition
  • Play/pause and skip reach the actual CD changer
  • TTS and alert sounds, Loxone-native when paired

zones & outputs

The speakers you already own are the system.

A zone is one device in one room. sonn speaks to nearly anything that can make sound — and a zone can also receive: point AirPlay or Spotify Connect at an old amplifier, and it just works.

Receivers
A receiver lets a phone play to a zone. Spotify Connect, AirPlay, DLNA and line-in land on the server, which sends them on to that zone's output — so a pair of Snapcast speakers on a Raspberry Pi shows up as an AirPlay target with no support of its own.
Grouping
Any rooms play in sync, regrouped on the fly. The kitchen joins the terrace for dinner and leaves again after — from the app, the wall panel, or a Loxone scene.
Power management
A zone can wake its amplifier before the first note and switch it off after the music stops — GPIO or USB relay, per zone or per shared amp group, with pre-delays and off-delays so short gaps never cycle the hardware.
Bang & Olufsen remotes
A Beoremote One can own a room: the server builds the menu it shows — favorites, radio, the turntable — and play, pause and skip route to whatever the room is playing.
This browser is a room
The web player registers itself as a local destination: select it and play, and it plays here — driven by exactly the same controls as the hardware zones.

loxone

A drop-in Loxone Audio Server.

Not an add-on that talks to Loxone — a complete Audioserver implementation. Pair it with a Miniserver and it behaves like the real thing: zones, sources, favorites, alerts and the native Loxone app, with up to 24 zones — the same ceiling as the original.

  1. Choose Loxone integrated under Zones — the server starts listening immediately.
  2. Add the Audio Server in Loxone Config with the serial and host from the Admin UI.
  3. Deploy and reboot the Miniserver. The icon turns green, and your zones arrive.

Non-Spotify services are presented to the Miniserver as virtual Spotify accounts — the only vocabulary Loxone clients have for them. Everywhere else they keep their own name. Disconnecting takes effect without a restart, and your own zones return.

home assistant & your own tools

And it plays well with the rest of the house.

An optional MQTT surface publishes every zone as retained topics — whole objects as JSON, single fields as plain scalars — so Home Assistant, Node-RED or a KNX gateway consume it with no code at all. Control works the same way: publish a value, done.

mqtt · retained
# state, retained — connect late, see everything
sonn/zones/3                {"name":"Kitchen","state":"playing",…}
sonn/zones/3/state          playing
sonn/zones/3/track/title    Song

# control — a value, or a relative nudge
mosquitto_pub -t 'sonn/zones/3/set/volume' -m '+5'
mosquitto_pub -t 'sonn/zones/3/cmd' -m '{"power":"on","volume":40}'
A native Home Assistant integration is on the roadmap. Today, HA already talks to sonn over MQTT and the HTTP API — the native component will make it one-click.

for builders

An API a curl one-liner is a first-class client of.

Commands are plain HTTP, and reading state never requires polling: subscribe to the event stream once and you are told about every change. No handshake, no correlation ids, no SDK to install — a five-line shell script is a complete integration.

curl — sonn /api/v1
# What is playing everywhere?
curl http://server:7090/api/v1/zones

{
  "id": 3,
  "name": "Kitchen",
  "state": "playing",
  "track": { "title": "Song", "artist": "Artist", "coverUrl": "…" },
  "output": { "protocol": "sendspin", "format": { "sampleRate": 192000, "bitDepth": 24 } }
}
# Pause the kitchen, then turn it down a notch
curl -X PUT http://server:7090/api/v1/zones/3/state  -d '{"state":"paused"}'
curl -X PUT http://server:7090/api/v1/zones/3/volume -d '{"volume":"-5"}'

# Say something in the kitchen; the music ducks and returns
curl -X POST http://server:7090/api/v1/zones/3/announce -d '{"text":"Dinner!"}'
# Follow every change — one subscription, no polling, ever
curl -N http://server:7090/api/v1/events

event: zone.changed
data: {"id":3,"state":"playing","volume":40,…}

# Every command is followed by an event — write, and watch it land.
# Realtime audio analysis, straight off the stream — up to 60 fps
curl -N 'http://server:7090/api/v1/zones/3/analysis?types=loudness,spectrum,pitch'

data: {"type":"spectrum","bins":[12,48,97,…]}
data: {"type":"pitch","midiQ88":17408}

# Build a VU meter, a wall visualizer, a beat-synced light scene.

Open by design

Apache-2.0, on GitHub. The public API is documented down to the field, and the web player is built on it and nothing else — if the contract can't carry a real player, that's a bug.

Events over polling

Two tabs, a wall panel and the Loxone app all agree, because everyone renders the same zone.changed. An edit made anywhere shows up everywhere, with no code of yours involved.

MQTT too

Same state, second surface: retained topics a Miniserver or KNX gateway can read without parsing JSON. Both run at once and cannot disagree — they publish from the same source.

get started

Running before the coffee is.

One container, host networking so it can discover your speakers, one folder that holds everything. Back up that folder and you have backed up the install.

quick start
# docker-compose.yml is in the repo — or:
docker run -d \
  --name sonn-core \
  --network host \
  -v $(pwd)/data:/app/data \
  ghcr.io/sonn-audio/core:latest

Then open http://<server-ip>:7090, create your admin account, and walk the three steps: Content — add your music. Access — switch on how to reach it. Zones — only if you want rooms. Steps one and two are already a working audio server.

Prefer bare metal? It runs directly on Node.js 20+ — clone, npm install, npm run build, npm start.
